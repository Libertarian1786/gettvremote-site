What TV Remote is

TV Remote finds a supported TV or streaming device on your local Wi-Fi network and lets you control it from your iPhone - power, volume, navigation and more, depending on what the device supports. It supports Roku players and Roku TVs, Amazon Fire TV (Fire OS, with ADB debugging turned on), Google TV and Android TV, Samsung TVs from 2016 on, LG webOS TVs and Vizio SmartCast TVs. Modern iPhones have no infrared transmitter; this app is not an infrared remote and does not control anything over IR.

Support for Google TV, Samsung, LG and Vizio is new in this version and not yet confirmed on every model. Not supported: Fire TV models running Vega OS (for example, Fire TV Stick 4K Select), which are not Android and do not support ADB, and Samsung TVs from 2014-2015, which use an older pairing method.

No guarantee every TV, network or firmware works

TV Remote is built against each platform's local control interface - the Roku External Control Protocol, the Android Debug Bridge (ADB) for Fire TV, the Android TV Remote protocol for Google TV, and the local remote-control interfaces of Samsung, LG and Vizio TVs - and checked against the devices, firmware and reference implementations available to us. We do not warrant that it will work with every model, every firmware or operating-system version, every router or network configuration, or that discovery or control will succeed in every case. Home network conditions - Wi-Fi client isolation, guest networks, VPNs, firewall rules - can prevent the app from finding or reaching a device that is otherwise supported. Some features depend on the TV itself: for example, turning a Samsung or LG TV on from the app is not possible, and volume through a soundbar depends on the TV's HDMI-CEC setting.

Pairing with your TV

Each TV decides which phones may control it. Depending on the TV you may choose Allow on the TV screen (Fire TV, Samsung, LG) or type a code the TV shows (Google TV, Vizio); an LG TV also needs its "LG Connect Apps" setting turned on. You can remove the app's access in the TV's own settings at any time; the app then stops controlling that TV until you pair again.

ADB debugging is your choice. Connecting to a Fire TV requires turning on Developer options and ADB debugging on that TV, and approving this app's pairing request on the TV screen. That is a developer setting you choose to turn on, at your own discretion, and you may turn it back off in the TV's Developer options at any time; doing so will stop this app - and any other ADB tool - from controlling that TV until it is turned on and approved again.

Free and Premium

The base app is free: it saves one TV and gives you the basic remote - power, volume, mute, the D-pad, OK, Back, Home, Play/Pause and channel keys, wherever the device supports them. Replacing or removing that one saved TV is free, and there is no daily or session limit on using it.

TV Remote Premium is an auto-renewing subscription that adds: unlimited saved TVs, Home Screen and Lock Screen widgets, Control Center buttons (iOS 18 and later), Siri, Shortcuts and Action Button support, the swipe touchpad, keyboard text entry, extra keys (rewind, fast-forward, instant replay, options) and color themes with a matching Home Screen icon. Premium never adds support for a TV brand or model the free app cannot already control.
